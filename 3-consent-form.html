Intended benefits

Sculptra is a collagen-stimulating treatment (poly-L-lactic acid, PLLA), not an instant filler. It works by prompting your body to build its own new collagen over time, gradually restoring volume and improving skin quality in the face or body/buttock.

What Sculptra is intended to do

  • Gradually build your own collagen to restore lost volume and improve skin firmness, texture and quality.
  • Provide a natural, gradual change — it cannot instantly fill like an HA filler, and cannot lift significant skin laxity or replace surgery.
  • The level of change achievable depends on your anatomy, the area, the amount used and your body's response.
  • Results appear gradually over about 6–12 weeks and build across the course.
  • Your outcome is assessed after the full course of sessions, not after a single session.
  • Results are long-lasting (commonly up to ~2 years) but not permanent.
  • Sculptra is given as a course of sessions plus maintenance top-ups over time.
  • Results vary between individuals and cannot be guaranteed.

Alternatives to this treatment

You do not have to have Sculptra. Reasonable alternatives were discussed with you, and you are free to choose any of them or none:

  • No treatment — choosing not to proceed at all.
  • Delaying the treatment to consider it further.
  • Hyaluronic-acid (HA) dermal filler — for a more immediate, reversible volume change.
  • Surgery or fat transfer — for larger-volume or lifting goals that Sculptra cannot achieve.
  • Skincare and lifestyle measures to support skin quality.
  • Referral to a specialist where appropriate.
  • Seeking a second opinion before deciding.

Risks & complications

No injectable treatment is entirely risk-free. Sculptra is well established and generally very safe, but the risks below may occur. They are grouped by how often they happen. Unforeseen risks may also occur that are not listed here.

Common

  • Swelling, bruising, redness
  • Tenderness & mild pain
  • Small early lumps or bumps
  • Itching at the injection sites
  • Temporary firmness in the area

Less common

  • Delayed-onset nodules or papules — small firm lumps appearing weeks or months later
  • Visible or palpable lumpiness / unevenness
  • Infection or abscess
  • Nerve irritation or altered sensation
  • Asymmetry or under-/over-correction needing further treatment

Rare but serious

  • Persistent or recurrent delayed nodules/papules needing treatment
  • Granuloma — a persistent inflammatory lump
  • Vascular occlusion (filler blocking a blood vessel) — rare with PLLA, but possible; can cause tissue damage
  • Serious or spreading infection
  • Allergic / hypersensitivity reaction
  • Scarring or skin colour change

Delayed-onset nodules & papules — the key Sculptra risk

The most important Sculptra-specific risk is delayed-onset nodules or papules — small firm lumps that can appear weeks or months after treatment. Most are minor and treatable when reported early; rarely they persist or recur and need further treatment. The risk is reduced by correct technique, correct dilution, and by you following the massage instructions (see below).

Your massage commitment (rule of 5s)

Massaging the treated area after Sculptra helps spread the product evenly and reduces the risk of lumps and nodules. The general rule is the "rule of 5s":

The rule of 5s

  • Massage 5 times a day
  • For about 5 minutes each time
  • For 5 days after treatment
  • Your practitioner's exact instructions take precedence, and the buttock/body protocol may differ

Emergency warning signs — act quickly

Contact Layali Clinic immediately on 07787 708200 if you develop:

  • Severe or rapidly increasing pain
  • White, grey, blue, purple or black skin
  • Blistering or skin breakdown
  • Rapidly increasing swelling
  • Spreading redness or heat
  • Pus or discharge
  • Fever or feeling severely unwell

Call 999 or go to A&E immediately if you develop:

  • Breathing or swallowing difficulty
  • Facial or throat swelling
  • Chest pain
  • Visual disturbance or loss of vision
  • Weakness, confusion, collapse or other neurological symptoms

Acting quickly matters — some complications are time-critical, so please do not wait. Out of hours, if you cannot reach the clinic, call NHS 111, or 999/attend A&E for anything severe or rapidly worsening.

Tell any other healthcare professional (A&E, GP, 111) the treatment you had, the product, the date and the injection/treatment site(s), and show them this consent so they can help you quickly.
